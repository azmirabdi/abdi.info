Why? Because in IT we slow the business down instead of speeding it up. We are the ones who tell our organisations that the next features will arrive in six months, while IT costs climb. The larger the organisation, the more tangled the IT becomes, until it takes a whole team of enterprise architects just to keep an overview.

**And why should DevDataOps make any of that simpler?**

Picture this. Cross-functional teams, each responsible for its own domain. They can specify, implement, test, and run their own requirements. They define their own professional language in a glossary of their own: their ubiquitous language, which reflects what they know about the domain. With DevDataOps they go one step further. They carry that language into an ontology of their own, in which the knowledge of the domain is represented.

And the useful part: that knowledge is not hidden. Anyone who needs insight (data) from it can request a data product by self-service.

The cross-functional team builds its own serverless algorithms. They do not only carry out the work in front of them. They also produce knowledge that is centrally available and immediately usable in later processes. No cumbersome data pipelines, no heavy data warehouses, no endless waiting for other systems, and no queue. What appears instead is a continuous, data-driven flow. That is what a modern, data-driven organisation needs. All of it rests on data-mesh principles, on a data-centric architecture, and on a knowledge graph following W3C standards. Let us give our organisations a lift again, instead of holding them back. Let us do DevDataOps.
