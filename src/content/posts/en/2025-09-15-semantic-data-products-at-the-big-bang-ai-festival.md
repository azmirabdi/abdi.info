On 11 September 2025 a water bottle stood on the table in Berlin. It was there to make a point.

At the Big Bang AI Festival, Christoph Koch and Tim Biedenkapp explained data products in a panel along these lines. The comparison below is rendered from the German account. It is not a transcript of their words. Raw data is water from a puddle. You can see it, and you still do not know what you are taking on. A data product is the bottle: a label, a cap, and a promise of quality that someone was willing to print because rules and standards sit behind it.

The picture was useful. We kept it for the masterclass, which is why the bottle is in the photo.

Inga Glotzbach, Johannes Meyer and I gave the session, the three of us from adorsys. The subject was semantic data products. The room was well filled, some people were taking notes, and it was fun.

How many customers did we have last quarter?

Sales counts leads that someone has contacted. Finance counts whoever has paid and is registered in the ERP. Support counts everyone who got in touch, whether they bought anything or not. Three numbers, three systems, one word. In our example an agent was asked how many active customers there had been in the previous month. It returned the wrong numbers. "Customer" is not one shared thing in the data, so the model guesses.

Semantics, here, means writing the meaning down and relating the products to each other. Who counts as a customer in finance, which transaction makes them one, which system holds the record, and where that touches the customer known to support. If a data product carries that with it, an AI system has a better chance of understanding the content than of guessing it. Whether that becomes value across the company depends on someone having asked, beforehand, the questions the product is supposed to answer. The slides call them competency questions. Which question must this data product be able to answer, and for whom? Earlier that year I had already described semantics, meaning shipped with the data, as one principle of a [data-centric architecture](/en/blog/2025/data-centric-architecture/). In Berlin it was a canvas that people from other companies filled in.

That was the first time we did it live with the participants: the Semantic Data Products Business Canvas, one field at a time. First on the example, then each person was to carry the step over to a case from their own company and tell the person on their left. Business outcome, competency questions, who uses the product, which ontology and which join keys connect the meanings, and how you can tell that it works. Plus the quality criteria, without which the bottle is only a shell: commitments such as SLAs and data quality, and the semantics that belong with them.

A data product described this way can be deployed on different platforms. We had already tried a few. The description has to survive the move, or the next platform starts again at the puddle.

I do not know whether the canvas lasted the train ride home. In the room it worked. Afterwards, "how many customers?" was at least uncomfortable enough that the first number on a dashboard was no longer an answer.
