What if we flipped the burden of delivery in enterprise architecture?

Picture a business domain that only had to describe which capabilities it provides and which outcomes it wants to achieve.

Everything else grows from a standardised, semantically described agreement: the Domain Contract.

The domain gets the adapters it needs to plug those capabilities straight into the enterprise architecture. Not only technically, but also for enterprise architecture management, business process management, analytics, governance, and AI.

Today it is often the other way around. Domains build their capabilities and then have to make sure their information is captured, documented, and kept up to date in all kinds of central systems.

Why, though?

Why doesn't the organisation provide a shared platform that gives domains the standards and tools they need — and that receives the information required for transparency, steering, and governance at the moment those capabilities are provided?

Domains stay autonomous. Their meaning and their capabilities become linkable through standardised contracts.

A federated approach, inspired by Data Mesh and Domain-Driven Design. In a [data-centric architecture](/en/blog/2025/data-centric-architecture/), a lot depends on meaning travelling with the work, instead of being chased down afterwards.

I call this idea Semantic Mesh.

Alongside project work, I have been looking closely for some time at how this could grow into an open, extensible architecture approach. Knowledge graphs are part of that for me: they make domain meaning structured and reusable, without forcing everything into a central monolith.

The idea is larger than a single tool or another platform technology.

I am working on turning these thoughts into a form that can be published.

More on this soon.
