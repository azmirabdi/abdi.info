Spoiler: it was an AI copilot.

What makes architecture documentation good?

- It stays **close to the code**.
- It lives **in the repository**, linked for example from the README.
- It is **written by the teams**, not handed down from above.

My architecture-documentation setup, a real onboarding manifest:

1. **Product vision**
2. **Requirements** (best as BDD features, including functional and non-functional requirements and a DDD glossary)
3. **High-level architecture** (context and component views, following C4)
4. **Architecture decision records (ADRs)**
5. **Coding guidelines** (naming, templates)
6. **Test strategy**

A side effect: if this documentation stays current (kept up through the definition of done and merge-request review), it becomes a real boost in productivity, for new colleagues **and** for your **AI copilots**.

I have been working with Cursor IDE (Anthropic) for a short while, and before that with GitHub Copilot and IntelliJ. What I have learned: **when the documentation is right, copilots deliver genuinely good results.** Suddenly ADRs are followed and conventions are respected, because the machine finally knows **how we think.**

Software engineering is changing quickly. Today we write documentation **not only for people**, but also **for machines**.

How do you document your architecture?
Do you keep it alive in the repo?
Do your copilots already use your README as the way in?

Share what you have seen. I am curious.
